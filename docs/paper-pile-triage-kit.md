# The pile is not a character flaw

Paper accumulates because every piece requires a decision: keep it, pay it, sign it, file it, call someone, or throw it away. When executive function is low, “I’ll deal with it later” is a completely understandable storage strategy. The problem is that later becomes a pile, and the pile becomes a source of dread.

The goal of this kit is not a Pinterest-perfect filing system. It is to reduce the number of open decisions in your home. You are allowed to have a messy drawer and a working system at the same time.

> [!NOTE] Sort before you organise
> Organising asks “Where should this live?” Triage asks “What is this and what happens next?” Do the second question first. Otherwise you will alphabetise the same unresolved paper three times.

## Five principles

1. **One armful at a time.** The whole pile is not a task. One armful is a task.
2. **Decide, don’t perfect.** A rough category is enough for today.
3. **Write the next action.** “Deal with insurance” is not an action. “Call the number on page 2 and ask whether the claim was received” is.
4. **Keep sensitive paper out of sight.** Do not leave account numbers, medical papers, or identity documents on the kitchen table when company comes.
5. **Stop when the timer ends.** A repeatable 15 minutes beats a heroic four-hour session that leaves you unable to face paper for a month.

## The only supplies you need

- A pen.
- A timer.
- A bin bag and a recycling bag.
- Five temporary boxes, trays, folders, or labelled spots on the floor.
- This printable.
- Optional: a phone for scanning, a folder for “needs action,” and a shredder or a plan for secure disposal.

You do not need to buy a label maker before you start. A sticky note counts as a label.

## If the pile is in several places

Start where you look most often. That might be the kitchen counter, the entryway, or the passenger seat of your car. Pick one. The other piles can wait their turn.

Turning over the page is the first decision. That is enough to begin.

# The five-box sort

Label five temporary spots. The names can be rough. The behaviour matters more than the label.

:::grid-3
### 1. Act now
Something with a real consequence in the next few days: a bill, a form with a deadline, an appointment letter, a renewal. Put it where you will see it, not in a drawer.

### 2. Date it
Something that matters later: a renewal notice, a ticket, a warranty, a seasonal notice. Write the date on the page or in your calendar.

### 3. File it
Something you may need again but do not need to act on: paid receipts, completed policies, closed accounts, reference letters.

### 4. Ask / verify
You do not know what it is, whether it is real, or what you are supposed to do. Put it in one place and write the question you need answered.

### 5. Release it
Obvious junk, duplicate copies, expired invitations, and anything you are genuinely finished with. Recycle the paper; shred what contains sensitive information.
:::

## The two-second question

For each item, ask only: **What is the next physical action?**

- If the answer is “put it in the action folder,” do that.
- If the answer is “write a date in my calendar,” do that.
- If the answer is “I don’t know,” put it in Ask / verify.
- If the answer is “nothing,” release it.

You do not have to complete the action during triage. Sorting is a separate job from doing. Trying to do both at once is how a 15-minute sort becomes a three-hour research project.

> [!WARNING] Protect sensitive documents
> Do not write full account numbers, government ID numbers, passwords, or medical details on this printable. If a document contains those things, move it to a secure place and record only the action you need to take.

# The 15-minute triage session

Use this page as your launch checklist. The session has three parts: set up, sort, and close. The close is what makes it repeatable.

## Set up · 3 minutes

- [ ] Clear a table or a patch of floor.
- [ ] Put the bin, recycling, and five boxes within reach.
- [ ] Put your phone on silent, or use it only for the timer.
- [ ] Fill a glass of water.
- [ ] Decide one stopping point: one armful, one counter, or one drawer.
- [ ] Set the timer for 15 minutes.

## Sort · 10 minutes

- [ ] Pick up one piece of paper.
- [ ] Ask: what is the next physical action?
- [ ] Put it in one of the five boxes.
- [ ] Do not open a new browser tab unless the paper cannot be understood without it.
- [ ] Do not start the action unless it takes under two minutes.
- [ ] Keep moving. Pauses to read every word are how the pile wins.
- [ ] When you find something emotional, put it in Ask / verify and keep sorting.

<!-- page -->

# Close · 2 minutes

- [ ] Put the File it box in its temporary home.
- [ ] Put the Act now papers where you will see them.
- [ ] Write the two or three actions you noticed in the action list on the next page.
- [ ] Move the Ask / verify pile to one folder or envelope.
- [ ] Put the Release bag by the door or in the bin.
- [ ] Write one sentence: what did this session actually clear?

# A file map you can actually use

A filing system works when you can find something without remembering where you put it. Broad categories are easier to maintain than a perfect taxonomy. Start with six folders or six hanging files. Add detail only if you keep missing things.

## The six starting folders

| Folder | Put this here | Keep in mind |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Home** | Lease or deed, utilities, home insurance, repairs, warranties | Keep current insurance and lease or deed in a secure place. |
| **Money** | Bills, bank letters, tax documents, receipts you need | Do not write account numbers on the folder label. |
| **Health** | Visit summaries, test results, insurance notices, receipts | Store privately. Share only with people who help with your care. |
| **Identity** | Passport, birth certificate, Social Security or local ID papers, immigration documents | Use a locked or fire-resistant place. Check official replacement rules before mailing anything. |
| **Work** | Contracts, pay records, training certificates, retirement paperwork | Keep confidential work material separate from household paper. |
| **Life misc.** | Pets, school, travel, memberships, instructions, warranties | This is allowed to be a catch-all. A real system needs one. |

# Physical or digital?

You do not need to scan everything. Scanning a whole archive can become another unfinished project. Choose by use:

- **Keep paper:** documents you are required to keep in original form, identity documents, and things you need to hand to another person.
- **Scan and file:** receipts you may need for reimbursement, instructions, warranties, and papers you want to reference but rarely need in original form.
- **Release:** duplicates, expired notices, and completed items that carry no continuing obligation.

## A folder name that helps

“Important” is not a useful folder name, because everything feels important when you are overwhelmed. Use the situation instead: “Home – current lease,” “Money – taxes 2026,” “Health – visits and results,” “Work – current contract.”

# Decode the envelope

The unknown is heavier than the task. Most mail becomes easier once you name what it is and what kind of response it needs. This table is a starting point, not instructions for your exact situation.

| What arrived | What it usually is | A sensible first move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bill or statement | A request for payment or a record of activity | Note the due date and the minimum action. Check it against what you expected before paying. |
| Insurance explanation of benefits | A statement of what was billed and paid — not always a bill | Check whether it says you owe something. If confused, call the number on the document. |
| Renewal notice | A reminder to renew or update something | Write the renewal date and the steps. Do not assume the price is unchanged. |
| Government form | A request for information or a notice of a decision | Read the deadline and the official contact channel. Verify the sender before sharing personal information. |
| Employment or benefits letter | A change, decision, or request for action | Note the date and who to contact. Keep a copy. |
| Legal or court notice | A document with legal consequences | Do not ignore it. Check the issuing office or seek qualified legal help. This kit cannot tell you what it legally means. |
| Collection notice | A claim that money is owed | Verify the sender and the debt before paying. Check official consumer resources or legal help if unsure. |
| Warranty or receipt | Proof of purchase or coverage | File it with the item’s name and date. You rarely need to act immediately. |
| Invitation or event notice | A date and RSVP request | Decide now: yes, no, or ask. Then release the paper. |
| Anything that feels off | Could be legitimate, could be a scam | Do not use the contact details printed only on the letter. Look up the organisation independently. |

## When you do not know what it is

Use the Ask / verify box. Write three things on a sticky note and attach it:

1. What I think this is.
2. What I need to know.
3. Who or where can tell me the answer.

That turns a mysterious document into a small research task. It also stops it from floating back into the pile without a next step.

## A phrase that saves time

“I received a notice and I’m not sure whether it requires action. Can you tell me what it is and what the deadline is?” This is a complete, respectful question. You do not need to apologise for not understanding the paperwork.

# Keep, scan, or shred

Paper decisions carry privacy and legal consequences. When in doubt, slow down. The safe answer is usually to keep the original until you verify the rule.

## Before you shred

- [ ] Is it definitely a duplicate or a completed item with no ongoing obligation?
- [ ] Could it be needed for taxes, insurance, employment, immigration, a warranty, or a legal matter?
- [ ] Is it the only copy?
- [ ] Have I checked an official retention rule if I am unsure?
- [ ] Does it contain an account number, ID number, signature, or medical detail?

If you are unsure about any answer, put it in Ask / verify instead of shredding.

## Sensitive paper

Identity documents, financial statements, medical records, and anything with an account number deserve more than the recycling bin. Use a shredder if you have one. If you do not, store the paper in a secure “to shred” bag and deal with it when you can, rather than leaving it visible in the bin.

Do not burn paper indoors or in unsafe conditions. Do not put sensitive documents in a shared recycling bin if secure disposal matters in your household.

## Scanning without turning it into a project

1. Choose one small stack.
2. Scan or photograph with a scanning app.
3. Rename the file with a date and a plain description.
4. Put it in the right digital folder.
5. Mark the paper for secure disposal or file the original if it must be kept.
6. Stop at the end of the stack.

A useful file name: `2026-03-14 home insurance renewal.pdf`. A file name like `Scan_00473.pdf` is technically a file, but not a system.

- Keep sensitive scans in a secure folder or encrypted storage if available.
- Do not send identity documents through ordinary chat or paste them into a consumer AI tool.
- Keep a backup of important digital files in a second place.
- Do not put passwords in the same folder as the documents they protect.

# When the envelope is the hard part

Sometimes the task is not sorting. It is opening. A letter can carry a bill, a diagnosis, a rejection, a demand, or a reminder of something you have been avoiding. Avoiding it is a nervous-system response, not a personality defect.

> [!NOTE] Open one. Do not solve one.
> You are allowed to open an envelope without handling the contents. The goal of this first step is information, not resolution.

## The lowest-barrier opening routine

- [ ] Sit somewhere comfortable with a bin and a pen.
- [ ] Put on a timer for five minutes.
- [ ] Open one envelope.
- [ ] Turn the letter face up and read only the first paragraph.
- [ ] Circle the sender and any date.
- [ ] Put it in Act now, Date it, or Ask / verify.
- [ ] Write one next action, or write “need help to understand this.”
- [ ] Stop.

If opening is still too much, ask someone you trust to sit with you while you do it. They do not have to read the letter. Their job is presence, not fixing.

## If the paper is about money

Shame makes bills heavier. Try to separate the facts from the story:

- Fact: “There is a balance due on the 14th.”
- Story: “I am bad with money and everyone knows it.”

Only the fact is useful for planning. If the amount is unmanageable, contact the organisation and ask about options before the deadline. A brief phone call or message can be more effective than another week of avoidance. For legal, tax, or benefits advice, use a qualified professional or official service.

# If the paper is about health or care

You do not have to interpret it alone. Write down the question and call the clinic, insurer, or person who sent it. If you are supporting someone else, ask them what they want to know before you start reading.

# The quarterly 15-minute reset

The point of a system is not to prevent paper from ever building up. It is to make the return trip short. Once every season, spend fifteen minutes checking the places where paper goes to hide.

## The quarterly checklist

- [ ] Clear the “Act now” papers that are already complete.
- [ ] Check the “Date it” box for dates that have passed.
- [ ] Move receipts and completed documents into File it.
- [ ] Look at the Ask / verify folder. Choose one question to resolve.
- [ ] Shred or securely dispose of the release paper.
- [ ] Check that identity and sensitive documents are in their secure place.
- [ ] Update the household command center if contact details or routines changed.
- [ ] Choose one thing to make easier next quarter.

## The one-file fix

If the system has collapsed, do not rebuild the whole filing cabinet. Make one “current admin” folder with five subdivisions: Act, Date, File, Ask, and Release. Keep it next to the place where mail lands. When it fills up, do another triage session. A system that can be restarted is more useful than one that has to stay perfect.

# Paper is a tool, not a report card

You will not be graded on the filing cabinet. The useful question is not “Is everything organised?” It is “Can I find the thing I need when it matters?” A messy system that lets you find the insurance letter is better than a beautiful system you are afraid to touch.

## The one-page takeaway

- Sort by next action before you try to organise.
- Use one armful and one timer.
- Put sensitive documents in a secure place.
- Keep the categories broad enough to maintain.
- Open one envelope before trying to solve one envelope.
- When unsure, verify before shredding.
