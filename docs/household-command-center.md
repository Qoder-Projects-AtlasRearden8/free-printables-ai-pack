# Build the hub before the hurry

Every household runs on invisible information: which bin goes out on Tuesday, who has the plumber’s number, how the cat takes his medicine, where the lease is, what to do when the power goes out. Usually one person holds it in their head. That works until they are sick, away, overwhelmed, or simply tired of being the household’s only search engine.

This is a reference, not a rulebook. It is designed to be filled in gradually and stored privately. You do not need to complete every page this week.

> [!WARNING] Keep this private
> A household hub can contain addresses, phone numbers, routines, and security details. Store it in a locked drawer, a private folder, or a secure location — not on a public fridge, in a shared cloud folder, or in a screenshot you text to someone. Never write passwords, PINs, security answers, or full financial account numbers on these pages.

## What belongs here

- Household contacts and local services.
- Who does which recurring tasks, and who can back them up.
- Home basics: utilities, shutoffs, maintenance, and where the manual is.
- Emergency plans and meeting points.
- Pet and plant care.
- Annual renewals and home admin dates.
- Where important documents live — a map, not a copy of everything.

## What does not belong here

- Passwords or password hints.
- Bank or card numbers.
- Government ID numbers.
- Full medical records.
- Anyone’s private information beyond what is needed for the household to function.
- A complete list of every possession for insurance unless you store it securely elsewhere.

## The update ritual

A household hub goes stale. Once a season, check the contacts, routines, and dates. Once a year, check the insurance, emergency plan, and document locations. Put those checks in the annual home admin calendar. An out-of-date hub is still better than none, as long as you know it needs a refresh.

# The daily and weekly minimum

Agree on a small list that keeps the household functional — not spotless. A minimum is easier to maintain than an ideal.

- [ ] Food is available.

## If the primary owner is away

Write down the things the backup would need to know: the grocery login? No. The store, the usual order, the pet routine, the school contact, the calendar. Keep it to information, not passwords.

# Emergency quick sheet

This page is a starting point for a household emergency plan. It is not a substitute for local emergency services or a doctor. Fill in your local numbers and check the plan with everyone who lives in the home.

> [!WARNING] Store this page safely
> Do not leave a page with names, phone numbers, medical details, and security information in plain sight. Use a locked drawer or a private folder. If you make a version for a sitter, leave out anything they do not need.

## In an emergency

1. Move to safety if you can.
2. Contact the local emergency number if there is immediate danger, a serious injury, fire, or a crime in progress.
3. Follow the instructions from emergency services.
4. Contact the household contact person when it is safe to do so.
5. Do not re-enter a dangerous building or try to fix a gas leak yourself.

# A starter list

Use what applies. Check official local guidance for anything safety-related.

- Test smoke and carbon monoxide alarms.
- Replace alarm batteries or backup batteries.
- Check the fire extinguisher.
- Clean or replace HVAC filters.
- Check the water shutoff and know how to use it.
- Check insurance renewal and coverage.
- Check vehicle registration or inspection dates.
- Review the emergency bag.
- Update the household hub.
- Back up important digital files.
- Clear the paper “act now” folder.
- Review subscriptions and annual renewals.

## The safety rule

Do not attempt repairs or maintenance that requires a qualified professional, especially gas, electrical, structural, or anything you are not trained to do. The calendar can remind you; it cannot make a dangerous job safe. Use the official utility or a licensed tradesperson.

# The ten-minute household reset

This is maintenance, not a deep clean. The goal is to interrupt the drift before it becomes a project. Choose one zone and one timer. Stop when the timer ends.

## The reset menu

:::grid-3
### Entryway
Shoes paired or put away, rubbish out, keys in their spot, one thing returned to its room. Stop there.

### Kitchen
Clear one counter, load or unload the dishwasher, wipe one surface, take out the compost or bin if full. Do not reorganise the cupboards.

### Bathroom
Clear the sink, replace the toilet roll, hang the towels, take out the bathroom bin. Stop before you start scrubbing the grout.

### Bedroom
Clear the bed, put one pile of clothes away, take out one glass, plug in the phone. The floor can wait.

### Living space
Clear one surface, fold one blanket, take out one cup, put remotes in their place. Do not start a full declutter.

### Paper landing zone
Pick up one armful, sort into act / date / file / ask / release, stop at the timer. The full triage kit has the details.
:::

## The rule of one

When the house feels impossible, do not make a list of every room. Choose the one zone that is causing the most friction: the kitchen counter before dinner, the bed before sleep, the entryway before leaving. A little friction removed from the place you use most is worth more than a perfect room you never enter.

# The digital backup check

- [ ] Important documents are backed up in more than one place.
- [ ] The backup is not only on the same device.
- [ ] The family knows how to access the backup if the primary person is unavailable.
- [ ] Recovery codes are stored securely, not in the same folder as the account.
- [ ] The plan has been checked in the last year.

## The loss or theft page

If a wallet, phone, or documents are lost or stolen, use official provider websites and phone numbers. You may need to contact the police, your bank, the identity document issuer, and your insurer. Do not share passwords or one-time codes with anyone who contacts you claiming to help. Record the reference numbers somewhere secure.

# A small hub is enough

The household will not become calm because one binder exists. It may become a little easier because the plumber’s number is not in your head, because the pet routine is written down, and because the person who usually handles everything has somewhere to point.

## The one-page takeaway

- Keep the hub private.
- Record where things live, not every private detail.
- Never write passwords or full account numbers.
- Make invisible tasks visible.
- Put annual checks in the calendar.
- Update the hub slowly; a partial hub is useful.
